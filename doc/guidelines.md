# Presentation and Discussion Guidelines

> Ported from the Canvas course page. This is the document that governs what is actually
> due for each class: role definitions, timing, and upload rules.

We will use a role-playing format inspired by Jacobson and Raffel. Our goal is to think deeply
about each paper and work through questions together. Each of us (the instructor included) may
understand only part of the work, but together we can build the full picture. As you read,
please consider the following guidelines:

- Identify the paper's key questions and answers.
- Consider the problem's challenges and how the approach addresses them.
- Examine the technical details closely.
- Note anything unclear for class discussion.

For each class, students will be assigned roles and discuss the paper from their assigned
perspectives. We will discuss two papers in each class. Depending on course enrollment, each
role may be filled by one student or a small group. Students will rotate through different roles
over the semester. The presenter will present the paper and also serve as its owner, responsible
for assigning roles and coordinating the presentation.

## Timing

| Who | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Presenter | 15 minutes |
| Each other role | 5 minutes |
| Per paper, total | ~1 hour (roughly 45 min presentation + 15 min discussion) |

Discussion can be mixed into the presentation or held at the end.

## Roles

### Presenter (Paper Owner)

You act like the paper's first author presenting it at a conference. Prepare a brief talk
(15 minutes) covering the motivation, problem, method, and results. You may use existing
materials, such as slides from the original authors.

As the presenter, you also serve as the paper owner and are responsible for selecting the paper
and assigning all other roles. Complete the following steps **at least one week before** the
presentation:

1. Select a paper and confirm with Qianqian (qwang@seas.harvard.edu). You can select a paper
   from the list of recommended papers, or propose your own; if you propose one, justify your
   choice.
2. Pick 5-6 roles that you think are most suitable for the paper you're presenting, and assign
   them to students by filling in the role assignment spreadsheet (second tab). Please assign
   roles with balance and diversity in mind. If students ask you for a particular role, you are
   welcome to accommodate them, but you are not obligated to do so if it complicates the
   logistics or works against the balance you are aiming for.
3. Announce the paper on Ed discussion. The announcement should include the title, author,
   abstract, and a link to the paper.

In class, the presenter leads the discussion: deciding the order in which each role presents,
keeping time, and asking questions to keep the discussion moving.

### Scientific Peer Reviewer

The paper has not been published yet and is currently submitted to a top conference where you've
been assigned as a peer reviewer. Complete a full review of the paper answering all prompts of
the official review form of the top venue in this research area (e.g., NeurIPS and CVPR). This
includes recommending whether to accept or reject the paper.

### Archaeologist

This paper was found buried under ground in the desert. You're an archeologist who must
determine where this paper sits in the context of previous and subsequent work. Find and report
on one older paper cited within the current paper that substantially influenced the current
paper and one newer paper that cites this current paper.

### Academic Researcher

You are a researcher starting a new project in this area. Propose an imaginary follow-up project
that is not just inspired by this paper but made possible by it. If the paper falls outside your
own area of research, you are encouraged to bring in your own research background when designing
the follow-up. State the research questions you aim to study clearly.

### Industry Practitioner

You work at a company or organization developing an application or product of your choice (that
has not already been suggested in a prior session). Bring a convincing pitch for why you should
be paid to implement the method in the paper, and discuss at least one positive and negative
impact of this application.

### Hacker

You are a hacker who needs a demo of this paper ASAP. Implement a small part or simplified
version of the paper on a small dataset or toy problem. Prepare to share the core code of the
algorithm to the class and demo your implementation. Do not simply download and run an existing
implementation, though you are welcome to use (and give credit to) an existing implementation
for "backbone" code.

### Red Team Critic

Your goal is to challenge the paper's approach. You may either demonstrate its fragility
empirically or advocate for an alternative method. For an empirical critique, identify inputs,
settings, or distribution shifts under which the approach degrades or fails outright. Try
out-of-distribution data, adversarial or edge-case inputs, component ablations, or
hyperparameters outside the reported range. Prepare to show the class one concrete failure and
explain what it reveals about the method's assumptions. Unlike the Hacker, you may use the
authors' official implementation, since your goal is to probe the method rather than reproduce
it. If you advocate for an alternative, contrast it with the paper's method and clearly explain
why it is preferable.

### AI Interrogator

You are an AI interrogator tasked with asking an LLM challenging questions about the paper and
critically evaluating its responses. You may probe its understanding, ask it to build on the
paper with new ideas, or explore other relevant questions. Identify what it does well, what it
misunderstands or overlooks, and any unsupported claims it makes. Share the prompts and
responses with the class, then present your own assessment of where the LLM succeeds and fails.

### Private Investigator

You are a detective who needs to run a background check on one of the paper's authors. Where
have they worked? What did they study? What previous projects might have led to working on this
one? What motivated them to work on this project? How does it contribute to their overall body
of work (maybe it doesn't)? Feel free to contact the authors, but remember to be courteous,
polite, and on-topic. Please do not depend on author response though. Please conclude with a
slide synthesizing explicitly what perspective each author brought to this paper, which others
couldn't bring (there is not always such contribution).

### Social Impact Assessor

Evaluate the paper's potential effects on society. What benefits do the authors identify? Are
there additional benefits they do not discuss? What potential harms, risks, or unintended
consequences might they have overlooked?

## Uploading Materials

All presentation materials must be uploaded to the shared folder **before class**. Each role
uploads one file, or a folder if you have multiple files, **named after the role**. Format is up
to you: slides, a webpage, or whatever works best.

Make sure your classmates can actually open your material:

- If you link to something outside the folder, check that the sharing settings let classmates
  view it.
- If you use Keynote or another Mac-only format, include a PPTX and PDF as well.

## Role assignment mechanics (from the assignment sheet)

Each paper presenter is responsible for selecting five or six roles appropriate to the paper and
assigning students to those roles. These roles are in addition to the presenter role, and each
may be filled by one or two students.

The class has been divided into two groups of approximately 20 students; see the third tab of
the sheet for the class roster and group assignments. The groups will alternate presenting. For
each class session, the two paper presenters should coordinate their assignments so that
students in the designated group are distributed across the roles for both papers. The goal is
for each student in the designated group to take exactly one role for one of the two papers.
