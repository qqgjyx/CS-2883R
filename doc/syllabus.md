## Course description

Computer vision asks how machines can perceive and make sense of the visual world. It is
a core component of artificial intelligence, and already underlies applications like medical
imaging, self-driving cars, and robots that work in warehouses. The course will cover a broad
set of research topics in computer vision and machine learning. Possible topics include 3D
vision, representation learning, generative modeling, world models, and vision-based robotics.
The format is discussion rather than lecture: we read recent papers together and students take
turns presenting and critiquing them from assigned roles. Students finish with a coding project
on an open research question.

## Course format and goals

The course consists mainly of research paper presentations and discussions. Attendance and
thoughtful participation are important and count toward the final grade. The first class
(September 9) and the first half of the second class (September 16) will be instructor lectures,
and the last is reserved for final project presentations. Every class in between will be
discussions covering two papers, following a role-playing format. There will also be three to
four short in-class quizzes.

Throughout the course, students will learn to read papers critically, present technical work
clearly, and argue productively about open problems. They will develop a working picture of the
current research landscape in computer vision and, through the final project, gain practice
taking a research question from formulation through experiment to a written result.
