D1 and D2: putting the model's own claims back to it with counter-evidence attached.
Both run as fresh Claude Opus 5 agents, no tools, no prior context.

--- D1 -------------------------------------------------------------------
Earlier, analysing RayZer (Jiang et al., 2025), an assistant argued:

"An objective invariant under any decoder-absorbable warp of the pose space cannot, in principle,
single out Euclidean camera geometry. That is a gauge freedom in the loss, not a shortfall in
optimization."

Here is a measurement from the same paper that the argument did not consider. Table 6 probes the
learned camera tokens by training a supervised 2-layer MLP head on a frozen camera-estimator
backbone. On RealEstate it recovers rotation to within 10 degrees for 99.6% of views, and
translation to within 0.1 for 61.2%. On DL3DV the same probe gets 47.6% and 20.8%. On Objaverse,
33.6% and 20.1%.

Does that measurement change the gauge-freedom claim? Answer directly: does the claim stand as
stated, need qualifying, or fail?

--- D2 -------------------------------------------------------------------
Earlier, analysing Table 6 of RayZer (Jiang et al., 2025), an assistant wrote:

"A supervised regressor that cannot place a camera on a known unit sphere to within 10 degrees four
times out of five is not evidence that supervision is hard. It is evidence that this particular
baseline is undertrained, misconfigured, or architecturally unsuited."

Two things about the paper bear on this. It never states the supervised baseline's training budget.
And it never defines the scale normalisation behind the translation thresholds t@0.1, t@0.2, t@0.3.

Given those two gaps, is "undertrained, misconfigured, or architecturally unsuited" a conclusion the
evidence supports? Or does the absence of those details mean the table cannot be read in either
direction? Answer directly.
